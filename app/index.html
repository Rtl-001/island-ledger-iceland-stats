    People

    Fewer than four hundred thousand residents share the island, and about two in three of them live in the capital region. The population is young by European standards and still growing, mostly through migration.

    Economy

    Fish, aluminium and visitors pay the island's way. Incomes sit near the top of the world table, unemployment is low, and prices — the ledger's sore line — run high.

    Health

    Icelanders are among the longest-lived people on Earth. Care is public and universal, infant deaths are rare, and the remaining health worries look familiar: weight, drink and long winter darkness.

    Life expectancy at birth, 2023

    Source: Statistics Iceland, life tables 2023.

    Education

    Schooling is free and compulsory to age sixteen, and nearly half of adults hold a university degree. Students abroad are common — the island exports its young and, increasingly, imports them back with qualifications.

    Land & energy

    A young island still being built: glaciers on a ninth of the land, some thirty active volcanic systems, and electricity drawn almost entirely from falling water and hot rock.

    Regions

    Eight statistical regions divide the island. One of them — the capital region — holds nearly two-thirds of everyone; the Westfjords hold fewer people than a single Reykjavík suburb.

    Sources & notes

    Every figure on this site is a published statistic, rounded for reading. The ledger's rule: no number without its source and its year.

    Where the numbers come from

    • Statistics Iceland — population, vital statistics, regions, education, trade and prices. The national statistical office; register-based counts.
    • World Bank — GDP per person in current US dollars, converted for the króna view at each year's average exchange rate.
    • National Energy Authority (Orkustofnun) — electricity generation by source.
    • Environment Agency of Iceland — greenhouse-gas inventory and land figures.
    • Icelandic Tourist Board — foreign visitors through Keflavík airport.
    • OECD — educational attainment and health-workforce comparisons.

    How to read this ledger

    • Reference year 2024 unless a figure names another year beside it.
    • Counts are rounded — thousands for population, one decimal for rates — so shares may not sum to exactly 100.
    • “Visitors” counts foreign arrivals through Keflavík airport, the island's main gateway; cruise and ferry arrivals are excluded.
    • Regional density divides the 2024 population by the region's land area, including uninhabited highlands, which is why it reads so low.
    • Life expectancy is period life expectancy at birth: the average years a newborn would live at 2023 death rates.

    The Island Ledger is an independent summary. Figures are rounded from the sources named on each page; for official series, consult Statistics Iceland.
